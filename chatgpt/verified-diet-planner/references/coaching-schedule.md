# Optional progress-coaching schedule

Use this workflow only after a validated plan has been delivered and the client explicitly accepts the offer of recurring check-ins.

## Offer and setup

End the plan with one localized invitation equivalent to:

> Would you like me to schedule regular check-ins to track your weight and progress and review whether your plan needs updating?

Do not create anything from the invitation alone. If the client accepts, ask only for missing details:

- cadence, such as weekly or every two weeks;
- preferred day;
- preferred local time and timezone when it is not already known.

Use the client's chosen cadence and time. Before creating the schedule, summarize it in one sentence. Create the automation only after any required connection or approval succeeds, and report success only when the automation result confirms it.

## Scheduled check-in content

Each check-in should ask for:

1. current weight and measurement date;
2. plan adherence, using a simple percentage or days followed;
3. average hunger and energy on a 1–10 scale;
4. training or activity changes;
5. sleep, recovery, digestion, or practical difficulties;
6. any new medical issue, medication, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or disordered-eating concern.

Compare progress over time only when prior measurements are available. Use the same units and prefer measurements taken under similar conditions. Do not interpret a single short-term fluctuation as a trend.

## Reviewing the plan

- If the client reports a new safety issue, pause nutrition changes and request clinician clearance.
- If progress is broadly on course and the plan is tolerable, recommend continuing without changing targets.
- If a change may help, explain the evidence and propose it; do not update calories, macros, or meals until the client confirms.
- A confirmed target change must repeat the applicable MyFitnessPal calculation, reconciliation, sourcing, and validation gates in the main skill.
- Preserve the existing plan as the baseline when no update is approved.

The schedule supports accountability and review; it does not imply continuous monitoring or medical supervision.
